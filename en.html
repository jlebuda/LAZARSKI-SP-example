What happens in two hours

You walk in and put on an apron. There’s clay on the table. You say what you want to make: a bowl, a mug, something you can’t name yet. One of us shows you how to start.

The first ten minutes are a bit awkward. That’s normal. After twenty your hands know what to do and the time goes somewhere.

Everyone in the room is busy with their own piece and nobody looks over your shoulder. After firing you come back for it. That’s the best moment.

Why it feels different here

  • Small groups

    As many people as one teacher can really teach. We teach in small groups because there’s no other way to teach.

  • Your own project

    There’s no set curriculum. You make what you want and pick up technique along the way. That’s why people stay a year without repeating anything.

  • A kiln on site

    Firings are frequent and your work never leaves the building. You collect it after 3–4 weeks. We don’t apologise for that. It’s how long firing takes.

  • Hand-building and the wheel

    Curious about throwing? There’s an answer. And a beginner has somewhere to progress to.

  • In Polish or in English

    We teach in English as readily as in Polish. The whole site works in both languages.

  • A teacher with a name

    Zuzanna teaches, and every teacher she adds stays a named person, not staff.
